      How the projection works

      Each player's value per game blends the last 15 days (half), the season (30%) and ESPN's projection (20%), with less weight on an average built from only a few games. Players listed out are left out until ESPN's expected return date and then counted at half value; questionable players count at half value for the next few days. When a teammate is out, a player's value goes up by their share of the minutes left behind (see News and opportunities). Each day uses the best lineup the slots allow from the players who have a game, and only starters count. ESPN locks each player at tip-off, so this assumes the lineup is set every day. It's an estimate to compare options, not a forecast to rely on.

          How the news is read

          When a regular (20 or more fantasy points and minutes a game) is out for the week, half of their fantasy production is shared among healthy teammates in the rotation, in proportion to each one's minutes and how closely their positions match. A boost can't take a player's value up by more than 30%. If the injured player has already missed most of the last 15 days, recent form already shows the effect, so the boost is halved. Boosts last until ESPN's expected return date and feed into the week projection and waiver suggestions.

          Each Rotowire note from the last 10 days is scanned for phrases about role (starting, more minutes, coming off the bench), returns and injuries, and for teammates it names as missing. The sentence that matched is shown so you can judge it yourself. This is pattern matching, not reading comprehension, so the odd tag will be off.

                      How trade ideas are found

                      Every roster in the league is projected day by day to the end of the fantasy season, using the same values and lineup rules as the week outlook, with injured players counted from ESPN's expected return date. One-for-one, two-for-one and one-for-two swaps with each team are tested. A team left with an open roster spot adds the best healthy free agent, and a team with a player too many drops its weakest. An idea is kept only if it raises your projection by at least 100 points and leaves the other team better off or within half a percent of where they were. In a two-for-one, the best player going the other way must be worth at least 65% of the one coming back, since few managers swap a star for two lesser players. The projections can't see team needs, keeper plans or how a manager rates their own players, so treat these as starting points for an offer.

                      How to read the status
                      Out
                      Listed out on the latest report found for the player's next or most recent game.
                      Questionable
                      Listed questionable or doubtful on the latest report found.
                      Day-to-day
                      Listed probable on the latest report found: likely to play, but still on the report.
                      Clear
                      Not on the latest report found (players listed as available count as clear).
                      Unconfirmed
                      No recent report was found either way. ESPN flags the player, but no NBA report from the last few days covers their team, so the flag can't be checked.

                      Statuses come from the NBA's official injury report where one covers the player's team. ESPN's own flags can lag, so they are only used when the NBA report has nothing to say, and a flagged player with no report to confirm it is shown as Unconfirmed rather than guessed. A red bar beside the slot marks a player who is out but sitting in an active lineup spot (anything other than Bench or IR).
